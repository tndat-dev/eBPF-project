\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A count language model over system-call tokens that keep error codes, returned sizes and the first directory of opened
files, counted per thread in the kernel and thresholded from normal behaviour only, detects 97.40\,\% of LID-DS~2021
attack recordings at 0.33\,\% false-alarm recordings, and in blind live runs on a Kubernetes cluster every
primary-rate attack with no idle false alarm, delivering alerts to Alertmanager within 0.68\,s at p95 for about
0.4--0.5\,\textmu s per watched system call. The same evaluation shows where it stops: it flags harmless new processes,
it is blind to attacks built from seen sequences and to data-only attacks inside a process, its threshold is a measured
knob rather than a guarantee, and its models age when the cluster changes. Separating a workload's own processes from
operator sessions in the kernel is the direction we take for the first of these limits.
A third live run after a cluster change replicates the results with a fresh fit and resists dilution, and the native
lineage stream flags 1/475 operator commands. On LID-DS~2019 the unchanged model detects 90.88\,\% of attack recordings
at 0.44\,\% false alarms, below our target, with Heartbleed and the dataset's attack timestamps as the limits.
