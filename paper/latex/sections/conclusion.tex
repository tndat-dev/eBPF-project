\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A count language model over system-call tokens that keep error codes, returned sizes and the first directory of opened
files, counted per thread in the kernel and thresholded from normal behaviour only, detects 97.25\,\% of LID-DS~2021
attack recordings at 0.19\,\% false-alarm recordings, and in four blind live runs on a Kubernetes cluster every
primary-rate attack; in the final 45-hour run it also catches every attack at 0.1 actions/s, on-off and diluted with
300 seen calls per operation, with no idle false alarm and p95 0.46\,s, for about 0.4--0.5\,\textmu s per watched
system call. The same evaluation shows where it stops: it flags harmless new processes, it is blind to attacks built from
seen sequences and to data-only attacks inside a process, its threshold is a measured knob rather than a guarantee, its
models age when the cluster changes, and its alert burden (0.36 episodes per replica-hour) is above the budget, mostly
in windows with exec-probe commands. Separating a workload's own processes from exec sessions in the kernel is the
direction we take for the first and the last of these limits: the native lineage stream flags 0/606 operator
commands. On LID-DS~2019 the configuration chosen on its development part detects 97.15\,\% of the remaining attack
recordings at 0.46\,\% false alarms, with the dataset's attack timestamps as the limit on latency.
