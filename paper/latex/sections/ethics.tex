\section*{Ethical Considerations}

\paragraph{Stakeholders and harms of the research.} All live experiments ran on a cluster the authors operate, with
synthetic traffic and no user data. The attack fixtures were written for this study; each one only affects its own
process or fails before changing host state, and no exploit of real vulnerable software and no vulnerable image was
deployed on the cluster. The public datasets (LID-DS~2021, LID-DS~2019, CB-DS) were used offline under their published
terms; they contain recorded system calls of the dataset authors' testbeds and no personal data. No human subjects took
part.

\paragraph{Harms of publication.} The paper documents blind spots of syscall-based anomaly detection: attacks built only
from seen sequences, data-only attacks inside a process, a gram map that can be filled, and a rule that downgrades
image-tool executions. These limits follow from known mimicry results and are needed by defenders to deploy such a
detector with the right complements (application-layer monitoring, durable alert queues, refit hygiene). We do not
release attack tooling beyond the self-contained fixtures used in the evaluation.
