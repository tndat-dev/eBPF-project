# Model design and score fusion for one-class syscall anomaly detection over heterogeneous feature groups

Research date: 2026-11-01. Scope: which learned model family, and which way of combining feature groups, lets a few informative new groups (file path, errno, I/O size, n-gram) drive detection without being drowned by ~250 count features, while keeping false alarms low and thresholds calibrated. Measured results are preferred over claims; "Inference" bullets are my reasoning, not sourced facts.

## Q1. Frequency-vector models vs sequence models for syscall HIDS: measured comparisons, cost, suitability for 0.5 s windows and live use

### Takeaway
Measured evidence does not show deep sequence models (LSTM/Transformer) clearly beating simple sequence statistics (n-gram/STIDE) at a fixed low false-alarm rate. Under leakage-free protocols a 3-gram matched or beat an LSTM on ADFA-LD at 1 % FPR and ran ~400x faster. Window-level frequency vectors fed to Isolation Forest / OC-SVM with normal-only thresholds did poorly on LID-DS 2021 (F1 <= 0.31 per scenario). Sequence context, especially per-thread, is where most of the signal is, which supports keeping n-gram information as its own detector rather than flattening it into the count vector.

### Cited Findings
- LID-DS 2021 is harder than LID-DS 2019: about 8.5x as many system calls, and the STIDE baseline gets a lower F-score, a lower detection rate and more false alarms on 2021. The recordings include syscall arguments, return values and metadata. — [Grimmer et al., Extended Abstract LID-DS 2021 (CRITIS 2022)](https://dbs.uni-leipzig.de/files/research/publications/2023-07/pdf/CRITIS_2022_Extended_Abstract_LID-DS-2021.pdf)
- On LID-DS (2019-era evaluation, seven algorithms, with and without thread information), BOSC and STIDE had the best detection rates (~98.6 %), while the autoencoder (AE) and MLP had the fewest false alarms. One STIDE setting reached DR ~0.98 with ~119 false alarms, and one MLP setting reached DR ~0.79 with ~4 false alarms. Thread information improved the MLP. — [Grimmer, Röhling, Kricke, Franczyk, Rahm: Improving Host-based Intrusion Detection Using Thread Information (EISA 2021)](https://old.dbs.uni-leipzig.de/file/EISA2021_Improving%20Host-based%20Intrusion%20Detection%20Using%20Thread%20Information.pdf). These figures come from a search-result summary of the PDF; I did not re-read the table myself.
- LID-DS 2021, frequency-vector one-class models: a 66-dimensional per-window feature vector (groups: unseen-argument and unseen-syscall influence, counts of negative return values, syscall frequency, maximum data volume for resource syscalls, PID/TID statistics, inter-event time histograms), with Isolation Forest and SGD One-Class SVM and thresholds calibrated to a target FPR on normal data only. Self-detection was weak: F1 <= 0.31 in all six scenarios at alpha = 0.05. Examples from Table 3: CVE-2012-2122 at alpha 0.01 had realised FPR 0.0099, precision 0.93, recall 0.064. CWE-89-SQL-injection at alpha 0.05 had FPR 0.051, recall 0.22. EPS_CWE-434 had FPR 0.090, recall 0.16. The authors write that "per-scenario one-class HIDS recovers only a small fraction of exploit windows when the threshold is not tuned on labels". — [Kozachok, Vyugov, Magomedov: From CVE to CWE: Syscall-Based HIDS Generalisation, arXiv 2606.22581 (June 2026, preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22581)
- Kozachok et al. also note that thresholds tuned on test data or labels inflate reported transfer. Their recommendation: "threshold calibration must be decoupled from exploit labels". — [arXiv 2606.22581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22581)
- LSTM system-call language model on ADFA-LD (Kim et al. 2016): three LSTM language models (1x200, 1x400, 2x400 cells), combined by a proposed ensemble of thresholding classifiers, reported AUC 0.928, above an averaging ensemble. — [Kim et al., arXiv 1611.01726](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01726). The 0.928 figure comes from a search snippet; I did not re-check it in the PDF.
- A leakage-free re-benchmark on ADFA-LD (normal-only training, thresholds set on held-out normal data at a target FPR) found the following. The 2-layer LSTM had the best AUC, 0.830 ± 0.004 over 3 seeds. At the operating point, the 3-gram unseen fraction had DR 0.170 at 1 % FPR, against 0.101 ± 0.013 for the LSTM. At 5 % FPR it was 0.327 against 0.273. The author notes that "AUC alone would have picked the LSTM". In streaming mode, the 3-gram had DR@1 % = 0.151 against 0.121 ± 0.044 for the LSTM, at ~1,500,000 syscalls/s on CPU against ~4,000 syscalls/s on GPU (~400x). On LID-DS 2021 CVE-2014-0160 with per-thread context, every model (3-gram, 6-gram, n-gram frequency, LSTM) reached DR 1.0 at 1 % FPR. With interleaved threads, the 3-gram and 6-gram had DR 0 and the LSTM had DR 1.0. — [saishyamdontha/syscall-ids-benchmark (GitHub, not peer-reviewed)](https://github.com/saishyamdontha/syscall-ids-benchmark)
- Transformer and Longformer language models for novelty detection in kernel traces reach "an F-score and AuROC greater than 95 % on most novelties" on a new dataset of more than 2 million web requests. The authors stress that large networks "typically require an enormous amount of data". The abstract gives no FPR or compute figures. — [Language Models for Novelty Detection in System Call Traces, arXiv 2309.02206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02206)

### Inferences
- Pulse's 0.5 s window and <= 2-3 s alert budget favour detectors that cost O(1) per syscall: n-gram lookups, count vectors, tree ensembles. A GPU-bound LSTM at ~4k syscalls/s (benchmark above) would not keep up with busy containers on CPU-only nodes. This is an inference from one non-peer-reviewed throughput number.
- The thread-interleaving result (n-gram DR 0 when interleaved, 1.0 per thread) suggests that per-thread n-gram novelty is a high-value feature group. Pulse's n-gram group should be keyed per thread, not per container. This is an inference from one scenario.
- The weak LID-DS 2021 one-class results came from a single flat 66-dimensional vector that mixed unseen-argument features with frequency counts. This is consistent with the dilution concern. It does not prove it, because the paper ran no per-group ablation of the model.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed head-to-head on LID-DS 2021 that compares LSTM, Transformer, CNN, frequency-vector models and n-grams under one normal-only threshold protocol. The CRITIS 2022 dataset report's full results table could not be read (paywalled at ACM).
- I found no measured per-window inference latency for deep syscall models at 0.5 s granularity.
- CB-DS and UNM comparisons were not found in this pass.

## Q2. Avoiding feature dilution across heterogeneous groups: per-group sub-models with combined p-values, conformal combination, subspace ensembles, weighting and selection

### Takeaway
The best-supported design is per-group detectors, each turned into its own conformal p-value, then merged by a fixed, pre-registered function that is valid under dependence. For sparse signals (one group fires), Bonferroni/min-p or Cauchy fits, and the two are asymptotically equivalent at small alpha. Fisher and the unadjusted harmonic mean should not be used: Fisher is provably anti-conservative with conformal p-values, and the HMP needs an e·ln K style multiplier. Subspace ensembles (feature bagging) exist precisely because full-dimensional detectors are diluted by irrelevant features.

### Cited Findings
- Feature bagging (Lazarevic & Kumar, KDD 2005, pp. 157-166) combines several outlier detectors, each run on a random feature subset (secondary sources give sizes between d/2 and d-1), to handle "very large, high dimensional and noisy databases". — [Semantic Scholar entry](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Feature-bagging-for-outlier-detection-Lazarevic-Kumar/e3116ebb52c152deae918a04c34441ac0d956b8a); [HighDimOut R doc (d/2 to d)](https://rdrr.io/cran/HighDimOut/man/Func.FBOD.html). The two sources differ slightly on the upper bound of the subset size.
- Combining detectors requires score normalisation, because raw outlier scores sit on different scales. Zimek et al. list sigmoid or mixture-model calibration, standard-deviation scaling and Kriegel et al.'s (2011) distribution-based unification. — [Zimek, Campello, Sander: Ensembles for Unsupervised Outlier Detection: Challenges and Research Questions (SIGKDD Explorations 2013)](https://kdd.org/exploration_files/V15-01-02-Zimek.pdf). Rank-based combination avoids the need to normalise. — [On the evaluation of outlier detection and one-class classification (Data Min. Knowl. Disc. 2023)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10618-023-00931-x)
- Conformal p-values are a principled normalisation. Each detector's score becomes a rank among calibration normals, so all groups share the Uniform(0,1) scale under the null. — [Bates, Candès, Lei, Romano, Sesia: Testing for outliers with conformal p-values, Ann. Statist. 51(1) 2023 / arXiv 2104.08279](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08279)
- Fisher's combination test is invalid on marginal conformal p-values that share a calibration set. At nominal alpha = 5 %, the type-I error is 20.5 % when gamma = 3 and approaches 50 % as gamma grows (gamma is the test/calibration size ratio). The 90th percentile of the conditional type-I error reaches 71.7 % at gamma = 3. "This can be easily fixed by suitably adjusting the critical value." Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) remains valid because conformal p-values are PRDS (Theorem 2). — [Bates et al., arXiv 2104.08279, Section 2, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08279). Note that this result is about combining across test points that share calibration data, not across feature groups of the same window.
- Merging functions valid under arbitrary dependence (Vovk & Wang, Biometrika 2020): 2 x arithmetic mean; e x geometric mean (the constant is <= e and there is "no point in improving" it for K >= 5); harmonic mean x a_K^H with a_K^H <= e·ln K (practical forms: 2·ln K for K >= 10, 1.7·ln K for K >= 50); K x min (Bonferroni). Under substantial dependence the (scaled) harmonic mean is the safer choice. A compound rule, 2·min(K·min p, e·geometric mean), performed well across their simulations. The merging function "must be made from prior or side information". Choosing it by looking at the p-values breaks validity. — [Vovk & Wang: Combining p-values via averaging (arXiv 1212.4966 / Biometrika 2020)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1212.4966)
- The unadjusted harmonic mean p-value (Wilson 2019) is anti-conservative unless a multiplier or threshold adjustment is used. Simes and the Cauchy combination give valid merged p-values for independent inputs. Chen, Wang & Wang recommend Simes or Cauchy over the HMP under dependence. — [Chen, Wang, Wang: Sub-uniformity of harmonic mean p-values, arXiv 2405.01368](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.01368)
- Cauchy combination (Liu & Xie) and HMP are both gamma = 1 heavy-tailed combinations. Under pairwise normal dependence (correlation not ±1) they are asymptotically valid as alpha -> 0 and "become equivalent to the Bonferroni test as the significance level tends to zero" (Thm 2.6). Power gains over Bonferroni are "subtle" for normal statistics and appear "particularly in situations where signals are dense". At alpha = 0.05 under independence, "only the Cauchy combination test can strictly control empirical type-I error". Under strong tail dependence (multivariate t, rho = 0.99, alpha = 0.05) the observed sizes were: Cauchy 0.0502, Bonferroni 0.0119 (conservative), Fisher 0.159 (inflated). With negatively correlated one-sided p-values, Cauchy can be more conservative than Bonferroni. — [Aggregating Dependent Signals with Heavy-Tailed Combination Tests, arXiv 2310.20460](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.20460)
- Measured calibrated fusion on syscalls: n-gram and LSTM scores were each converted to tail p-values against held-out normal data and fused by the sum or max of -log p, with the fusion rule fixed before running. Result: "no fusion of n-grams and LSTM beat the 3-gram at the declared operating point" on ADFA-LD. The 3-gram had test DR@1 % = 0.165. Max(3-gram, LSTM) had 0.123, and the LSTM alone had 0.106. — [syscall-ids-benchmark (GitHub, not peer-reviewed)](https://github.com/saishyamdontha/syscall-ids-benchmark)
- Feature filtering on normal-domain stability can destroy transferable signal. "Importance-driven selection with a controlled shift penalty is preferable." — [Kozachok et al., arXiv 2606.22581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22581)

### Inferences
- **Recommended structure (inference):** keep the existing 249-count reference tree as one group (G0). Add K small group detectors (path novelty/density, errno pattern, I/O-size distribution, per-thread n-gram, exec/process). Each produces a conformal p-value p_g from its own nonconformity score on the shared calibration windows. Merge them with one pre-registered function, for example Bonferroni-Cauchy: p = min(1, K·min_g p_g), or the Cauchy statistic T = Σ w_g·tan((0.5 - p_g)π). With K = 5-8 groups, Bonferroni costs only a factor 5-8 in alpha. Because Cauchy ≈ Bonferroni at small alpha (2310.20460, Thm 2.6), the choice matters little at Pulse's per-window alpha. Then apply the existing k2of2 temporal rule to the merged p.
- This structure guarantees that a single strong group (p_g ≈ 1/(n+1)) cannot be diluted by 249 quiet count features, because it never shares a model with them. Inside one ExtraTrees over 400 features, by contrast, a new feature only matters if splits pick it, and the prediction error is dominated by the many count targets (inference about tree behaviour, not measured here).
- Fisher or a sum of -log p rewards dense agreement and is the wrong shape when the expected signal is sparse (one group). Max/min-p is the right shape for "a few informative features should drive detection". This follows from the dense/sparse power discussion in 2310.20460 and from Vovk & Wang.
- Weights w_g in the Cauchy statistic, or an alpha split per group (weighted Bonferroni), can encode prior importance. They must be fixed before the test data is seen (Vovk & Wang's no-peeking condition). They can be learned on development scenarios only.
- Within one window, the group p-values are dependent (the same activity drives several groups). Bonferroni, Cauchy (asymptotically) and the Vovk-Wang scaled means stay valid under that dependence. Fisher does not.

### Gaps
- I did not read the full Lazarevic & Kumar paper. Its exact dilution experiments and accuracy numbers are unverified here.
- I found no syscall-HIDS paper that measures per-group conformal p-value fusion against a single flat vector on LID-DS. That comparison would be new (a possible paper contribution).
- Mixture of experts and learned gating for one-class detection were not researched in this pass.
- I did not fetch the Vovk-Wang work specifically on combining conformal p-values (as opposed to generic p-values), nor conformal e-value methods.

## Q3. Ensembles of per-signal learned detectors vs "rules"; calibrated combination of anomaly detectors

### Takeaway
The defensible distinction is this: each sub-model learns a score or density from normal data and is calibrated to a stated false-positive rate, and the fusion is one pre-registered statistical test with a known global error rate. A rule uses a fixed hand-written criterion ("unseen = alarm") with an ad hoc limit. Honestly, though, a Bonferroni/min-p fusion of novelty detectors is mathematically an OR of calibrated detectors. The scientific gain over the rejected k-of-n voting is calibration and a controlled family-wise FPR, not a different decision shape.

### Cited Findings
- A merging function is "an increasing function of the p-values whose output is still a valid p-value under any dependence". Merging can be layered (group -> global), at some cost in conservativeness. — [Vovk & Wang, arXiv 1212.4966](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1212.4966)
- Conformal p-values make arbitrary detectors comparable and turn any score into a test with a marginal FPR guarantee. Bates et al.'s techniques also "yield a uniform confidence bound for the false positive rate of any outlier detection algorithm, as a function of the threshold applied to its raw statistics". — [Bates et al., arXiv 2104.08279 (abstract)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08279)
- Outlier-ensemble literature treats combining heterogeneous detectors as a calibration and normalisation problem (Kriegel 2011 unification, Aggarwal & Sathe bias-variance framework). — [Zimek et al. 2013](https://kdd.org/exploration_files/V15-01-02-Zimek.pdf); [Aggarwal & Sathe, Outlier Ensembles (book 2017)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/315871214_Outlier_Ensembles)
- Kozachok et al.'s 66-feature vector already contains "unseen-argument and unseen-syscall influence" features learned against normal data inside a one-class model. Rule-like novelty signals are routinely used as learned features in recent HIDS work. — [arXiv 2606.22581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22581)

### Inferences
- To be "ML, not rules" (inference about how reviewers will read it), each group detector should (a) be fitted on normal data, as a density, a regression or tree-based nonconformity, a frequency-weighted novelty, or a kNN distance, rather than a binary set-membership test; (b) output a graded score; (c) be converted to a conformal p-value. The fusion then uses one declared alpha. A binary "unseen path" test has a degenerate score distribution: p is 1 when the path was seen and ≈1/(n+1) when it was not. That is what makes it look like a rule. Graded versions (rarity weighted by the frequency of the path prefix in training, or the likelihood under a smoothed n-gram model) behave as learned detectors.
- A second distinction: the fusion can include G0 (the existing reference tree). An alarm can then come from count anomalies, from argument anomalies, or from moderate evidence in several groups at once (Cauchy/HMP can capture several moderate p-values; min-p cannot). k-of-n voting over binary votes cannot express that.
- The measured syscall fusion (n-gram + LSTM via -log p) did not beat the best single detector. Fusion is not free: it adds multiplicity cost. Group detectors should be added only when they bring signal that the others miss (evidence: LID-DS v4 ablation in the repo, not this research).

### Gaps
- I found no literature that formally defines "rule-based vs learned" for HIDS novelty detectors. The distinction above is my argument.
- Laxhammar & Falkman's inductive conformal anomaly detection (ICAD) was not fetched in this pass.

## Q4. Threshold setting: max-score-on-validation vs conformal p-values vs quantiles; small validation sets; conformal under drift

### Takeaway
The LID-DS library threshold is literally "the maximum anomaly score seen on validation". That is a conformal threshold at alpha ≈ 1/(n+1) with no tunable alpha and high variance from the single extreme order statistic. Conformal p-values with an explicit alpha give the same exchangeability guarantee, plus a known FPR distribution (Beta), and they allow per-group alpha and correct fusion. Small calibration sets make the realised FPR noisy: its relative spread scales as (n·alpha)^-1/2. Under drift, weighted or adaptive conformal methods are needed.

### Cited Findings
- LID-DS `MaxScoreThreshold`: the threshold starts at 0 and is replaced by any larger validation score ("maximum anomaly score of validation data as threshold"). A score is anomalous if it is strictly greater. If no validation score is positive, the threshold stays at 0. — [LID-DS/LID-DS max_score_threshold.py](https://github.com/LID-DS/LID-DS/blob/master/algorithms/features/impl/max_score_threshold.py). The repo uses the same rule in `/home/tndat/Downloads/eBPF-project/scripts/analysis/lidds_dl_baselines.py` and `/home/tndat/Downloads/eBPF-project/sentinel_pulse/lidds/signals.py` (function `thresholds`).
- For marginal conformal p-values with calibration size n, the realised FPR at level alpha follows Beta(l, n+1-l) with l = floor((n+1)·alpha). The relative spread (coefficient of variation) of the FPR is ≈ proportional to (n·alpha)^-1/2. With 100 calibration points, FPR(0.1) > 0.15 has non-negligible probability. Even with 1,600 points, FPR(0.01) > 0.015 is "still quite possible". "Inferences based on marginal conformal p-values may be systematically invalid for a large fraction of practitioners working with 'unlucky' calibration data sets." — [Bates et al., arXiv 2104.08279, Prop. 1 and Section 5.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08279)
- Calibration-conditional valid (CCV) p-values hold the FPR with probability 1-delta over the calibration draw, via a Simes-type adjustment h. With n = 1,000 and delta = 0.1, the smallest p-value becomes ≈ 4.61/n ≈ 0.0046 (instead of 1/(n+1) ≈ 0.001), and a marginal p of 0.025 becomes 0.0377. An asymptotic version gives ≈ 4.09/n. — [Bates et al., arXiv 2104.08279, Section 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08279)
- Normal-only, FPR-calibrated thresholds on LID-DS 2021 realised FPRs above target in some scenarios. Bruteforce_CWE-307 at alpha 0.01 realised 0.0315, and EPS_CWE-434 at alpha 0.05 realised 0.0899. The authors note that calibration "assumes that the calibration normal is representative of operational normal". — [Kozachok et al., arXiv 2606.22581, Table 3 and Section 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22581)
- Under covariate shift, weighting calibration points by the likelihood ratio restores validity (weighted conformal). — [Tibshirani, Barber, Candès, Ramdas: Conformal Prediction Under Covariate Shift](https://candes.su.domains/publications/downloads/WeightedCP.pdf). Under general non-exchangeability, the coverage gap is bounded by weighted total-variation terms, with recency weighting as a practical choice. — [Barber, Candès, Ramdas, Tibshirani: Conformal prediction beyond exchangeability (Ann. Statist. 2023)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/371558117_Conformal_prediction_beyond_exchangeability). For online shift, adaptive conformal inference adjusts alpha_t from feedback. — [Gibbs & Candès: Adaptive Conformal Inference Under Distribution Shift (NeurIPS 2021)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/352054520_Adaptive_Conformal_Inference_Under_Distribution_Shift). Weighted conformal p-values for outlier and selection testing under covariate shift: [Jin & Candès, arXiv 2307.09291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09291)
- Weighted conformal anomaly detection pays a resolution-variance cost: large importance weights create large steps in the p-value distribution. — [Between Resolution Collapse and Variance Inflation, arXiv 2603.23205 (2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.23205)

### Inferences
- The max-on-validation threshold amounts to alpha ≈ 1/(n_val+1) per scored unit. With few validation windows, the threshold is set by a single extreme, so false alarms on test are very sensitive to whether one noisy validation window was present (CV ≈ (n·alpha)^-1/2 = 1 at alpha = 1/n). This explains why max-validation thresholds behave erratically across scenarios. The inference follows from Bates Prop. 1.
- **Sizing (inference):** for 10 % relative spread at alpha = 0.01, n·alpha ≈ 100, so n ≈ 10,000 calibration windows. LID-DS recordings of 10-60 s at 0.5 s give ~20-120 windows each. With tens of validation recordings that is a few thousand windows, and those windows are autocorrelated within a recording, so the effective n is smaller still. Practical consequences: calibrate at the recording level (max p-value per recording) or use CCV-adjusted p-values. Pool calibration across workloads only when each workload's own p-values are checked for uniformity. Per-group alpha should not be tiny (alpha_g ≥ 10/n).
- The k2of2 temporal rule already lowers per-window FPR. Its effective alpha depends on window autocorrelation, so it should be measured empirically on held-out normal recordings, not derived assuming independence.
- For Pulse live (drift from deploys and traffic changes), the cheapest supported option is a sliding or recency-weighted calibration set (Barber et al.). ACI-style alpha adaptation needs label or feedback signals that a HIDS does not have online.

### Gaps
- I found no empirical study that compares max-validation, quantile and conformal thresholds on LID-DS specifically.
- I did not research conformal validity with autocorrelated windows (block or recording-level exchangeability) in depth.

## Q5. Per-workload vs global models; data needs with ~200 training recordings of 10-60 s per scenario

### Takeaway
The evidence is thin, but it points to per-workload (per-scenario) normal profiles, with pooling only across workloads whose normal behaviour is a superset. Transfer between profiles is strongly direction-dependent. Deep sequence models are data-hungry, while n-gram, tree and frequency models fit comfortably in LID-DS-sized training sets.

### Cited Findings
- Cross-CVE transfer inside one CWE class is direction-dependent: Bruteforce_CWE-307 -> CVE-2012-2122 gave F1 0.805, and the reverse gave 0.078. Transfer is "governed by the breadth of the source normal profile rather than by the shared CWE label". A combined CWE-307 normal profile was the strongest detector: F1 0.6976 at alpha 0.05 with realised FPR 0.0702, and precision 0.964 / recall 0.437 at alpha 0.01. The same protocol failed for CWE-89 and CWE-434 (F1 <= 0.21). — [Kozachok et al., arXiv 2606.22581](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22581)
- Large neural language models for kernel traces "typically require an enormous amount of data to be trained effectively". The authors built a dataset of more than 2M requests for that reason. — [arXiv 2309.02206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02206)
- On ADFA-LD, 833 normal training traces were enough for a 3-gram to match or beat a 2-layer LSTM at 1 % FPR. — [syscall-ids-benchmark](https://github.com/saishyamdontha/syscall-ids-benchmark)

### Inferences
- With ~200 recordings of 10-60 s per scenario, per-workload ExtraTrees and group detectors (n-gram tables, path/errno/size distributions) are well within data limits. Training an LSTM or Transformer per workload is not justified by any measured gain found here. A global sequence model with per-workload conformal calibration is a possible middle ground, but I found no evidence for it on LID-DS.
- Per-group detectors need per-workload calibration, because path and I/O-size vocabularies are workload-specific. Kozachok's results show that one profile applied to another workload's normal data can raise "essentially everything above threshold".

### Gaps
- I found no LID-DS study comparing global vs per-scenario models under one protocol.
- I found no learning-curve study (detection vs number of training recordings) for syscall HIDS.
